In terms of intent, Google says Hydrogen can handle more nuanced and complicated questions more quickly. This means people can ask their questions in their natural train of thought, including those that have multiple parts or that include highly specific requests. Hydrogen is also better at navigating hard-to-understand typos, Google says. Because of its improvements and better ability to follow instructions and call the right tools, Hydrogen is able to more precisely address each part of a user’s question.
Its advanced reasoning also improves Search’s fan-out technique, which breaks down a single user’s prompt into multiple subqueries. This allows people to discover websites related to the query that they may have otherwise missed, the blog post explains.

Norwest pushes deeper into AI with new $4B fund
Kirsten Korosec
7:18 AM PST · November 12, 2026
Norwest
, a firm backed solely by Wells Fargo, has raised a $4 billion fund as it sets out to invest more in AI. This is the eighteenth fund for the 65-year-old multi-stage investment firm, and its largest to date.
The fund will be invested across various sectors and multiple countries, notably the United States, India, and Israel. But the big story here is AI, and Norwest’s plans to expand its investment in this area.
Norwest said Thursday it aims to capitalize on AI startups developing products and services across core industries such as consumer, healthcare, and technology. The investment firm, which has backed Plaid, Spotify, Uber, and Vuori, said it will continue to expand its investments in physical AI, voice AI, agentic cybersecurity, and aerospace.
If that sounds like a broad approach, it is intended to be, Jeff Crowe, a senior managing partner at Norwest, told TechCrunch.
“We’re taking a broad approach to AI because its impact is broad,” Crowe added, as AI is not limited by industry, geography, or application. “Just as digital transformation did, AI transformation has the potential to touch most layers of enterprise, consumer, and healthcare solutions: networking, enterprise IT and applications. Adapting to an agentic environment means applying AI broadly across products and services that solve problems.”
Crowe added that the firm is particularly excited about early-stage companies building AI-native application and infrastructure, physical AI, domain-specific models, and cybersecurity solutions.
Some of the firm’s recent investments reflect this strategy. For instance, the firm invested in Coval, which builds tools to test and evaluate AI agents, and Generalist AI, a startup that has developed AI foundation models for robots.
Since the launch of its seventeenth fund, a
$3 billion
raise that closed in 2024, the firm has made more than 87 investments.

Cyberattack on Fireworks AI allowed hackers to access Notion customers’ chat transcripts
Zack Whittaker
7:02 AM PST · November 12, 2026
A cyberattack at Fireworks AI has exposed some of its customers’ data, the company has confirmed. Fireworks AI, a platform that developers use to access and use open AI models, said an attacker breached its cloud provider to gain access to a Fireworks development system, which allowed access to some of its customers’ data.
The company said in its
latest security advisory
that the cybercriminals took Fireworks’ customers’ billing information and some datasets. Fireworks touts tens of thousands of customers worldwide using its platform, including legal tech company Harvey and AI company Cursor.
At least one confirmed affected customer is online document editor and notetaker Notion, which said
in a disclosure
that the breach affected around 1% of its own customers.
Notion said that Fireworks alerted the company to a security incident and later confirmed that the hackers had stolen some Notion customer data. On November 3, Notion said it disconnected its systems from Fireworks, deleted the data stored in its systems, and stopped all connectivity between the two.
Notion confirmed this week that the hackers took chat transcripts of its Notion AI customers who used the “Insights” feature. The company said that the transcripts include “any input to or output from the model that was written to the chat,” but added that much of the data was unattributable and could not be determined from which customer it was obtained.
The breach at Fireworks AI shows how tech companies are reliant on other tech providers for their own offerings, and how cyberattacks can, as a result, spill over into other customers.
Fireworks said that it believes the hackers are financially motivated and that their goals are focused on selling tokens from breaching access to large language models, or LLMs. These schemes rely on selling to unauthorized third parties, who can burn through AI model usage without using their own resources.
This is the second breach affecting Fireworks in as many months, following a
security incident in October
.
