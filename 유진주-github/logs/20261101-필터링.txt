Apple CEO says the company doesn’t ‘want to ship AI for AI’s sake’
During
a recent interview with Wired’s Steven Levy
, Apple CEO John Ternus defended the company’s current position in the AI landscape.
Over the past couple years, Apple has often been described as behind on AI, especially as it delayed the release of a more powerful version of Siri, which
finally launched this summer
. But while Ternus — a longtime Apple executive who
officially became CEO
at the beginning of September — acknowledged that “everyone has an opinion,” he insisted, “I really like where we’re at.”
Ternus added that Apple is “absolutely going to leverage AI” in its new products, but he said, “We don’t want to ship AI for AI’s sake. When we’re doing it right, people won’t have to think about whether it was AI that made this happen or more traditional software. What matters is, did we build something that they really love?”
And when Levy pressed him on whether AI might affect Apple and its ecosystem more dramatically, with agents potentially making things like apps unnecessary, Ternus replied,”That could make life really easy, I suppose, but the creation of products is a deeply human and personal thing, something that we love to do.”

Judge allows NAACP lawsuit over xAI data center pollution to move forward
Anthony Ha
2:17 PM PDT · October 31, 2026
A federal judge denied a motion by xAI seeking to dismiss
an NAACP lawsuit
accusing the SpaceX-owned company of failing to obtain permits needed to operate the gas turbines powering its Colossus data center outside Memphis.
Suing on behalf of the NAACP, the Southern Environmental Law Center said the AI company had “installed and operated at least 35 combustion turbines and other sources of air pollution at the Colossus site without ever obtaining the necessary preconstruction or operating air permits.” It also accused xAI of violating the Clean Air Act.
xAI sought to have the lawsuit dismissed,
claiming that the turbines are exempt
from permitting requirements because they’re mobile. The company also argued that the plaintiffs did not have the standing to sue.
“Because the plaintiffs have statutory standing under the Clean Air Act, because the combustion turbines are not ‘mobile sources’ under Mississippi’s [State Implementation Plan], and because the citizen suit provision does not violate Article II of the Constitution or the private nondelegation doctrine, the plaintiffs’ claims will proceed,” Judge Debra M. Brown wrote in
her decision
.
Focusing on xAI’s claim that these kinds of citizen lawsuits are unconstitutional, Judge Brown wrote, “Because citizen suit plaintiffs sue on their own behalf, in their own name, and seek penalties that redress harm to themselves, they are not exercising the federal government’s executive power.”
TechCrunch has reached out to SpaceX for comment.

a statement
, the NAACP’s director of environmental and climate justice Abre’ Connor described this as “another important victory in our effort to hold xAI accountable.” (A similar motion to dismiss from the Department of Justice was
denied earlier this month
)

OpenAI reportedly lowers funding target for new round
OpenAI’s latest funding round isn’t quite living up to expectations, according to multiple news reports.
OpenAI was previously reported to be
raising $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation
, and then
at a $1.25 trillion valuation
, ahead of a planned IPO. But
The Financial Times reported Friday
that the company has subsequently lowered its funding target to $20 billion, with a first close that’s expected to be even lower than that.
The round will be led by a group of investors from the United Arab Emirates, the FT said. A subsequent retail offering for wealthy individuals could bring the total funding amount to the $20 billion target.
A
follow-up story in The Wall Street Journal
offered more details, namely that the company has secured $11 billion so far, and that its fundraising efforts had met with a “tepid” response from some large investors.
TechCrunch has reached out to OpenAI for comment. The company’s reported funding challenges could be blamed, in part, on bad timing, as
Wall Street investors have become wary of heavy AI infrastructure spending
, leading to a major sell off of tech stocks. And Anthropic’s planned IPO could be
facing challenges
, too.
