Russell Brandom
10:56 AM PDT · October 12, 2026
Meta’s Superintelligence Lab released
a pair of new state-of-the-art models
on Monday, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, representing the company’s first attempt at a frontier model.
Where Muse Spark aimed to be a lightweight model that could perform at low latency, the Wonder models are larger and said to be more capable, with higher benchmark scores and better results on complex tasks like coding.
According to Meta’s testing (presented in
the model card
), Wonder and Wonder Full achieve comparable benchmark scores to Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra.
“Together, they mark the next step on our scaling ladder and a major leap toward personal superintelligence,” Meta’s blog post about the models reads.
That scale is reflected in the pricing: Accessed through the Meta Model API, Wonder will cost $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, making it roughly equivalent in cost to GPT-6 Sol or Opus 5.5.
Notably, Wonder Full will only be available to Muse Code subscribers and will not be served through the API. Both models will also be used to power the Muse agent.
In keeping with Meta’s interest in developing personal assistants that can perform tasks over a long period, the company placed particular emphasis on the new models’ ability to retain and synthesize information given by the user, a property Meta calls “active memory.”

the MemoryBench benchmark
, which challenges continual learning and memory construction frameworks. According to the company’s testing, Muse Wonder now scores higher on MemoryBench than Fable, Opus, or Astra.

WindBorne wins a hefty US Air Force contract for its balloon data
Tim Fernholz
10:19 AM PDT · October 12, 2026
The U.S. Air Force will buy data collected by
WindBorne Systems
‘ fleet of autonomous weather balloons under a new contract that could be worth nearly $50 million to the startup.
WindBorne currently has about 700 weather balloons flying around the globe, collecting atmospheric sensor readings that the company sells to customers and also feeds into its own AI weather forecasting model. WindBorne, founded in 2015 and based in Redwood City, California, is one of the first organizations to directly feed empirical data into a deep-learning weather model.
The company has worked closely with the U.S. government, particularly the National Weather Service and the U.S. Air Force and Navy, but this is the single-largest contract the company has participated in. The contract is worth up to $49 million, but the Air Force pays only as it needs data. It can place orders for specific regions at specific times. So far, it has ordered $1.7 million worth, and executives expect more orders to follow.
“This is largely a vehicle through which continued growth of the constellation can be supported, as well as some work on research and development to continue to improve the hardware and performance of the balloons,” WindBorne co-founder and CTO Andrey Sushko told TechCrunch. He added that the contract, which was awarded without competitive bidding, is “a determination by the government that for this capability of collecting global data, we were the only company able to provide it in the way that met their needs for these operations.”
