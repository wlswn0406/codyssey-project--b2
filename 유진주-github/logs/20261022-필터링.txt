Recursive’s automated research system, which it calls the Eureka Machine, helped with nearly every step of Flint’s development, the company says. That let a small team build the model from scratch in under four months.
Flint is an early public test of recursive self-improvement: the idea that AI can build better AI, which in turn builds even better AI. It’s also one of the most debated ideas in the industry. Some researchers fear that AI improving itself could slip out of human control. For now, Recursive’s version is more limited: an AI research assistant overseen by people that produced a model small enough for a laptop.

$650 million in funding

roughly 70% of the websites

The NSA warned that these holes are so severe that if a website can’t be patched immediately, it should be pulled off the web until it can be. The holes allow attackers to infiltrate the server’s file system or a database that may be used by the web app.

The recommendation is to update the web server to the current version of PHP, which is 8.5.12.

in a blog post
. Separately, the Wiz team also used AI, namely its Wiz Atlas security AI agent, Gemini 4 Argon, and GPT-5.6 Sol to find multiple vulnerabilities in PHP.
Both OpenAI and Wiz followed responsible security research practices and worked with PHP on patches before publicly disclosing the flaws. Then they all worked together to flesh out what they found, and test the patches, drawing in a host of others to help.

Blacksmith hits unicorn valuation after Sequoia leads Series C
Blacksmith, a platform that helps companies more efficiently build and test software, including code written by AI,
announced Wednesday
a
$66 million Series C
funding round led by Sequoia that values the company at $1 billion.
In announcing the round, Blacksmith co-founder and CEO Aditya Jayaprakash said the company was founded to make continuous integration (CI) — the process of building, testing, merging, and validating code changes —
“less painful.”
In a blog post, he said that “most companies are struggling to keep up with the volume of CI they need to run,” especially in an era where so much code is written by AI.
“As agents write more of the world’s software, the question that matters is whether it can be trusted,” he added.
The company also announced that its annual recurring revenue has grown 17x and that it has run over 90 million CI jobs, or individual automated code checks. Customers include Mercury and Granola.
The Series C
comes just two months
after its $45 million Series B, which valued the company at $550 million. A year ago, Blacksmith was valued at $60 million after raising a $10 million Series A. It has raised $125 million to date.
