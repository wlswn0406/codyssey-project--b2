Ex-Palantir execs launch Dream with $17M from a16z to build AI world models for construction
Marina Temkin
9:21 AM PDT · October 29, 2026
Andy Elder and Arjun Mathur were Stanford roommates who both studied computer science before spending a decade at Palantir. Elder led product design for a team of 800 people building software for U.S. defense and intelligence agencies, while Mathur served as a senior vice president in the company’s commercial practice. When the friends decided to build a startup early last year, there was no question they would do it together.
Despite having no direct background in architecture, Elder and Mathur were troubled by a growing problem: Fewer buildings are constructed in the U.S. today than a
few decades ago
, as development costs have surged.
“We’ve gone from dial-up internet to superintelligent AI, but our ability to build the physical world around us has actually decreased,” Mathur told TechCrunch.
Believing AI can boost productivity across the architecture, engineering, and construction (AEC) industry, they set out to build
Dream
, a startup developing built world models that automate building design and engineering. Dream has emerged from stealth with $17 million in seed funding led by Andreessen Horowitz, alongside other backers, including SV Angel, XYZ, and Zigg Capital. The startup aims to replace cumbersome, outdated AEC tools like Autodesk’s AutoCAD and Revit.
If you sit next to an architect or a structural engineer, you’ll notice that they are still “drawing” every piece of their designs part by part on legacy software, Mathur said. “AI really plays no role in that equation.”
While a standard LLM like ChatGPT can generate an image of a building, the result won’t be structurally sound. Dream, by contrast, is grounded in real physics and construction standards. Just as LLMs learn from text, Dream’s world model is trained on more than 100 million square feet of building data gathered from permit archives across the United States.

Wabi, the AI agent that builds mini apps, now valued at $1.25B after latest round
Sarah Perez
7:35 AM PDT · October 29, 2026
The race to build the winning AI assistant is on, and investors are spreading out their bets. On Thursday, another AI app,
Wabi
, now describing itself as a “personal agent,”
said
it has raised $55 million at a $1.25 billion valuation.
Founded by Eugenia Kuyda, who previously founded the AI companion startup Replika, Wabi originally focused on allowing anyone
to build apps using prompts
. Last month, however, the startup
announced
a pivot of sorts to become an AI messenger.
The idea now is that the new Wabi — or Wabi 2.0, as it was called — could build the interfaces you need to get things done as part of its role as your personal AI agent.
The company suggests Wabi could be used to build mini apps for things like meal planning, tracking local restaurants, planning date nights, creating fitness plans, designing specialized trivia games, and more.
Image Credits:
Wabi
The additional funding indicates that Wabi’s investors agree that its new agentic angle has some potential, especially given the market around AI agents is hot right now, with viral AI agent Instinct pulling in another billion
at a $10 billion valuation
in September.
Even Tolan, which makes a cute, Muppet-like AI companion to help people navigate life,
just raised $30 million
.
“Eugenia has a unique sense for where AI is headed and a track record of great taste in building it,” said Alex Lim, general partner at IVP, which led the new round, in a statement. “She has a clear vision for how people will interact with AI, one that goes far beyond the text box or command line. Wabi is the expression of that vision, and we’re thrilled to support her.”
Other participants in the round included Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), Forerunner, Amazon Alexa Fund, and angel investors like Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke, Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff, former Yahoo! head Marissa Mayer, former Twitter CEO Dick Costolo, and one half of The Chainsmokers, Alex Pall.

Deepgram acquires Pipecat-maker Daily to bolster its voice AI infrastructure
Ivan Mehta
6:00 AM PDT · October 29, 2026
The voice AI sector is hot, and top players are trying to consolidate their position as more business models come to the fore through partnerships and acquisitions. Deepgram is the latest to make such moves: The company said on Thursday that it is acquiring Daily, the maker and maintainer of one of the most popular open source voice AI infrastructures, Pipecat.
The companies didn’t disclose the deal amount but said that they will keep Pipecat open source.
For the uninitiated, Pipecat is a framework that manages voice and video streams along with other pieces in the pipeline, like speech-to-text (STT), large language models (LLMs), and text-to-speech (TTS) models while reducing latency. Its maker, Daily, provides hosted infrastructure for voice and video calls of any kind.
With Pipecat, Deepgram will likely gain a deeper understanding of real-time voice and video infrastructure.
Daily has raised over $62 million in funding to date from investors such as Tiger Global, Highfield Capital, Nat Friedman, Lachy Groom, and Kevin Weil, according to PitchBook data. Earlier this year, Deepgram reached unicorn status with
a $130 million Series C round
. Incidentally, both companies were in the same Y Combinator batch in 2016.
Kwindla Kramer, Daily’s co-founder, said around the time when GPT-4 came out, the company started experimenting with AI-to-human communication over voice and text, which eventually resulted in the Pipecat framework. He noted that while there has been a lot of acquisition interest, he favored Deepgram’s CEO Scott Stephenson’s commitment to keeping Pipecat open source forever, and the engineering culture of both organizations matched, giving Daily’s team the freedom to work on different parts of the stack within a bigger organization.
