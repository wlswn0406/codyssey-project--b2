Replit is no longer working with Anthropic, CEO says
Lucas Ropek
2:22 PM PDT · October 16, 2026
Replit, the AI coding platform that has become a favorite of startup founders and developers, is no longer working with Anthropic, the company’s CEO and co-founder Amjad Masad said Thursday while onstage at
TechCrunch Disrupt
.
Masad said the decision was made for a number of different reasons, including cost. Replit
announced
a partnership with OpenAI in August, but there was never any indication that it was no longer with Anthropic.
“I think ChatGPT is better now,” Masad said, adding that the company uses an internal benchmark to help assess metrics like performance. “We publish a public benchmark as well, and consistently, Sol and Astra perform better on building vibe-coded applications from scratch. It was a decision based on both cost, partnership, and performance. And for all these reasons, we just felt like they were a better partner for now.”
Replit sells a cloud-based software development platform where users can develop and deploy apps directly from inside their browser. Under its partnership with Anthropic, Replit used the AI lab’s Claude models to run its platform. That partnership was said to have
helped
the company grow its revenue significantly.
Masad’s comments were rejected by insiders familiar with the partnership between the Anthropic and Replit, however. One person familiar, who asked not to be named, said the partnership was still in place.
There is evidence on
the company’s website
, blog posts, and social media posts that Replit was using Anthropic products as recently as a few weeks ago. For instance, a
blog post
in late September cites Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5 and Claude Fable 5.1 as models running in Replit Agent’s production environment. Around the same time, one of Replit’s product employees
posted
on X about using Anthropic’s flagship model Opus 5.5 and promoting its capabilities.
TechCrunch reached back out to Replit for clarification and will update this story if we hear back.

India becomes the latest country to report rogue AI agent activity
Aditya Mehta
10:52 AM PDT · October 16, 2026
AI agents from multiple providers accessed personal data and confidential material at Indian organizations during research and testing, India’s cybersecurity agency
said
Thursday.
The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) said it had received “multiple voluntary disclosures from AI model providers” whose models, during research and evaluation runs on the open internet, “had reached the live systems of Indian organizations.”
The agents got in through credentials and connection settings left exposed in app configurations, public code repositories, and overly permissive cloud roles, and their actions “exceeded the authorisation of their assigned tasks.”
This makes India the latest stop on what has become a world tour for rogue AI agents. In July, OpenAI’s agents escaped a test environment and
hacked French company Hugging Face
— kicking off a summer of rogue AI agent activity. Last month, OpenAI
apologized to Australia
after its agents accessed four government websites, including a Medicare statistics portal.
At Hugging Face, OpenAI’s agents started the same way but then found and exploited software flaws to run code on the company’s servers. CERT-In described no such hacking in India, where the agents appear to have simply used the access left lying around.
Still, the Indian cases appear to be worse in one respect, which is what the agents saw: In Australia, OpenAI said it found
no evidence
its agents touched individuals’ medical or criminal records, even after they wrote files inside a Medicare statistics system, while in India, agents read personal data and confidential business records. And unlike earlier incidents, each disclosed by a single lab, India’s case involved agents from multiple providers.
CERT-In did not name the providers or affected organizations, or say whether the agents altered any systems. TechCrunch has reached out to CERT-In for more information.

Elorian’s first AI model wants to reason with images, not just about them
Aditya Mehta
9:01 AM PDT · October 16, 2026
Elorian
, the Palo Alto AI startup that raised a $55 million seed round at a $300 million valuation before it launched a product, released its first model on Friday, TechCrunch has exclusively learned.
The model,
Glimpse
, is launching as a research preview with API access and a chat interface is waitlist-only, so for now it’s a results table and a thesis. The thesis? Today’s AI models, for all their skill with language and code, still can’t really see.
Most multimodal models look at an image once, convert it to tokens, and then reason about it in text — like describing a photo from memory. Glimpse keeps going back to the source. Mid-reasoning, it can crop into a region, pull specific frames from a video, or run code to measure or compare what it’s looking at, a mechanism Elorian calls a “visual scratch space.” Think of someone pinching to zoom on a phone photo to double-check what they thought they saw.
For former Google DeepMind researcher and Elorian CEO Andrew Dai, one of the clearest signs that AI still couldn’t really see came on game night.
Last year he photographed a board game he’d just finished with friends and asked a frontier model who was winning. It couldn’t say. Board games “each have their own visual language, each have their own iconography, symbols,” he told TechCrunch. “To an adult, it’s quite easy to understand what’s going on with minimal text, but these models aren’t able to intuit what the symbols mean.”
“Humans can look at Ikea diagrams, with no text, and assemble Ikea furniture, right? Why can’t AI models?” he said.
The approach isn’t completely new — OpenAI added crop-and-zoom during reasoning to its o-series models in 2025 — but Elorian’s pitch is that it built the whole model around it, cheaply. The company says a small team trained Glimpse on a few hundred GPUs and that the model is substantially smaller than the frontier systems it’s benchmarked against.

Jev is Vercel’s fastest-adopted AI model ever
Russell Brandom
8:33 AM PDT · October 16, 2026
Jev has become the fastest adopted AI model ever — at least by some measures.
Vercel data
published on Thursday
shows that, within two weeks of its launch,
the buzzy decision model
was being used by 35% of paid teams, with nearly double the reach of the second most popular model, Sonnet 5.5. It’s the fastest adoption a model has ever seen in its first two weeks, according to Vercel’s data, and a major accomplishment for the model’s creators, TypeSafe AI.
Published in limited release on September 15, Jev is designed as a lightweight tool for choosing between a few predetermined outputs. Limiting the outputs lets the model run cheaper and faster than alternatives like Sonnet, making it ideal for classifying large quantities of data quickly. That new approach has attracted significant buzz in the weeks since Jev’s release — inspiring competing offerings from
OpenAI
and
Amazon
, among others — but until now there has been little hard data to back up the good vibes.
The data comes from Vercel’s
AI gateway
, a unified API service similar to OpenRouter. It’s a necessarily limited view — particularly at risk of undercounting major labs like OpenAI and Anthropic that offer their own APIs — but it shows the buzz around Jev really did translate into actual usage. In fact, Vercel was an early adopter of Jev, using it to replace GPT-6 Luna for classifying harmful commands.
The report also shows continuing momentum for open source models, which now account for more than 70% of the tokens processed through Vercel’s gateway and nearly 25% of the total spend.
While open source models are capturing a larger share of the market, the market is also growing fast. Spending on frontier labs has grown tenfold, even as it accounts for a smaller share of overall spend, giving credence to the idea that frontier models and open source alternatives may turn out to be
complementary rather than competitive
.
