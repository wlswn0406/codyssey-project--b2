Apple is reportedly cutting budgets for its internal AI usage
Apple is cutting the daily amount its engineering teams can spend on AI, Bloomberg
reported
Wednesday. It’s a big change for a company that has worked
to integrate
the technology into its engineering processes.
In October, Apple lowered the daily AI spending cap for its engineering teams, decreasing it from $300 to $200, the outlet reported. And more cuts are in the works, with some allowances falling to $100 a day, sources tell Bloomberg. Outside engineering, Apple is reportedly weighing a daily limit as low as $25 a day.
TechCrunch has reached out to Apple for comment.
Earlier this year, a
tsunami of tokenmaxxing
took hold, with companies pushing staff to use AI as much as possible in the name of vaguely defined efficiency gains. The frenzy was short-lived, however, as bills for said tokens came due, and companies decided that token rationing might be a more fiscally sensible approach.
Concerns persist
about an apparent gap between what AI costs to build and run and the (often unclear) productivity gains the technology is suppose to generate.

AI PACs spent millions trying to shape Congress — here’s how they fared
Rebecca Bellan
2:45 PM PST · November 4, 2026
Political groups both supporting and opposing the AI industry spent millions of dollars trying to influence Tuesday’s midterm elections and shape the future of AI regulation. That spending produced mixed results, with some of the most heavily backed candidates losing their races.
On the industry side, two AI-focused political action committees (PACs) that raised and spent money to influence the elections — American Mission and Building the Future — collectively spent $4.8 million to back South Carolina Senate candidate Darline Graham, who lost. American Mission is the Republican spending arm of
Leading the Future
(LTF), a broader political group backed by OpenAI president Greg Brockman and the VC firm Andreessen Horowitz.
American Mission also spent more than $500,000 apiece supporting Republican House candidates Aaron Flint in Montana and Laurie Buckhout in North Carolina, both of whom lost. LTF’s Democratic spending arm, Think Big, had better luck backing Florida Rep. Jared Moskowitz, who won reelection.
Groups advocating for stronger AI safeguards also saw mixed results.
Public First,
which is backed by Anthropic, supported several Republicans who lost through its affiliated PAC but had more success backing Democratic candidates. It also contributed $3 million to a PAC supporting California state Sen. Scott Wiener, who was leading his race at the time of publication.
Meanwhile,
Guardrails Alliance
, a group that has sought to make taking money from AI companies a political liability, spent more than $1 million apiece opposing two candidates who lost: California Rep. Jimmy Gomez and Montana’s Aaron Flint. It also spent $300,000 supporting Angela Gonzales-Torres, who defeated Gomez.

Sarah Perez
1:56 PM PST · November 4, 2026
Very few people are using personal AI agents to order Ubers, apparently. On the transportation giant’s
third-quarter earnings
call on Wednesday, the company said that only four rides were ordered through an AI agent during its busiest week in October: the week of Halloween.
While more people used an AI agent to order food through its delivery service, Uber Eats, the volume was still fairly small compared to the overall number of trips.
Uber’s chief financial officer, Balaji Krishnamurthy,
told
Wall Street analysts on the call that the company handled around 320 million trips and orders across Uber and Uber Eats during the week of Halloween, but only 500 Uber Eats orders were placed by an AI agent.
“It is very, very early and very limited traction that we are seeing so far,” said CEO Dara Khosrowshahi, referring to the low number of orders placed by AI agents. “Listen, we hope that there will be more traction.”
The surprisingly low numbers of AI-initiated transactions come amid growing hype for a new wave of AI agents that don’t just answer questions but also handle tasks on users’ behalf. There’s been an explosion of personal AI startups, including one of the buzziest, Instinct, which was founded last year and raised its most recent round of funding at a
$10 billion valuation.
Meta’s Muse also became the No. 1 app in Apple’s U.S. App Store last month, topping 10 million users,
per Meta’s own reporting
. The company said many were using it to shop and transact with businesses.
OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and SpaceXAI (Grok) are also now offering their own AI agents, as are
numerous smaller startups.
Still, early numbers from public companies indicate the agentic web still has a long way to go. DoorDash, for instance, said last week on its earnings call that it has
only received a few hundred orders
from AI agents so far, as most customers are still placing orders through the DoorDash app.

new essay

He lays out five policy priorities, spanning worker protections and education to AI safety and data center development.

OpenAI’s policy agenda last year,
which focused on accelerating AI development, reducing regulatory barriers, and expanding infrastructure.

The original New Deal established protections for workers and placed constraints on corporate power. It’s less clear how Lehane’s vision would hold AI companies accountable for the economic disruption their technology could create.
