Google expands AI Search ads globally as core ad growth slows
Lauren Forristal
2:48 PM PDT · October 28, 2026
Google is bringing ads in its AI-powered Search experiences to international markets, betting that generative AI can open up new revenue opportunities even as growth in its core search advertising business slows.
The announcement came Wednesday during Alphabet’s
third-quarter earnings
call, as the company reported $127.6 billion in revenue, up 25% year over year. But Search advertising growth continued to decelerate, and Alphabet shares fell 8% following the results.
Google Search revenue grew 12% year over year in the third quarter, down from 17% growth in the second quarter and 19% in the first quarter. The slowdown puts added pressure on Google to turn its expanding AI features into a meaningful source of advertising revenue.
“We are now scaling high-quality ads within AI overviews in AI mode to more users and queries,” Philipp Schindler, Google’s chief business officer, said during the call with investors. “This includes taking our monetization footprint global for the first time. Today, we’re announcing the expansion of ads in AI mode into international markets alongside multi-turn ads and AI mode in the U.S.”
Google says early results suggest the formats are working. Click-through rates for sponsored product listings in AI Mode are already comparable to those for organic listings, according to Schindler. The company’s user research also indicates that people find the ads relevant.
The move builds on Google’s May
announcement
of new Gemini-powered ad formats, including conversational discovery ads that respond to users’ questions and sponsored recommendations integrated into AI-generated answers.
Google CEO Sundar Pichai also said that Search queries grew at their fastest year-over-year rate in five years, though he did not disclose the exact growth rate. He credited AI features with changing how people use Search, including prompting longer and more complex queries.

Microsoft recorded $10 billion in revenue from OpenAI
Rebecca Bellan
2:34 PM PDT · October 28, 2026
Microsoft generated $10 billion in revenue from commercial arrangements with OpenAI, including revenue-sharing payments, in the quarter ended September 30, the company said in a
regulatory filing
on Wednesday. That represents nearly 11% of Microsoft’s total quarterly revenue of $92.3 billion, highlighting the enormous financial ties that remain between the two companies even as they increasingly
compete
for the
same customers
.
(Microsoft’s financial year begins in July, making the quarter ended September 30, 2026 the first quarter of its fiscal 2027.)
For the full 2026 fiscal year, revenue from OpenAI hit $24.1 billion. At its latest quarterly pace, Microsoft would pull in $40 billion annually from OpenAI-related arrangements.
Microsoft also reported $7.2 billion in outstanding receivables from OpenAI at the end of September, up from $6 billion at the end of June. That money represents amounts OpenAI owes for previous transactions, not additional revenue.
It’s not clear how much of Microsoft’s
revenue from OpenAI
came from revenue-sharing payments or Azure cloud computing services. TechCrunch has reached out for more information. In November 2025, TechCrunch
reported
, citing leaked documents, that Microsoft had received $865.8 million in net revenue-sharing payments from OpenAI during the first nine months of that year.
OpenAI
reportedly
shares 20% of its revenue with Microsoft as part of a previous deal in which the software giant invested more than $13 billion in the AI startup. (Neither the AI lab nor Microsoft have publicly confirmed this percentage.) As part of an April 2026 agreement, OpenAI is on the hook to continue sharing revenue with Microsoft through 2030, subject to a cap, even as OpenAI is allowed to work with competing cloud providers.
