Meta removes language that raised scraping and AI training concerns
Meta recently updated its commercial terms of use, declaring that the company can “use any content from [a business account’s] domains, web pages, or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of” Meta products and services.
According to
legal experts interviewed by Nieman Lab
, this looked like a “remarkable provision” that could give Meta permission to scrape the website of any publisher with a Meta business account and use the content to train the company’s AI models. (While
Meta defeated a high-profile lawsuit over alleged copyright infringement
last year,
other cases
against AI companies, including Meta, continue.)
However, a Meta spokesperson
insisted
that the updated terms did not reflect a change in the way Meta crawls the web. And now,
The Verge reports
that the provision has been removed.
“We’ve heard you and want to clarify: the crawling terms we consolidated only applied to our ads and business products, and we’ve clarified that in the terms themselves,” the company said in a statement. “It does not change how Meta uses web crawlers.”

Replit makes OpenAI its default over Anthropic, CEO says
Kirsten Korosec
Lucas Ropek
Rebecca Bellan
11:48 AM PDT · October 17, 2026
Replit, the AI coding platform that has become a favorite of startup founders and developers, is no longer working with Anthropic, the company’s CEO and co-founder Amjad Masad said Thursday while onstage at
TechCrunch Disrupt
.
Masad said the decision was made for a number of different reasons, including cost. Replit
announced
a partnership with OpenAI in August, but there was never any indication that it was no longer partnering with Anthropic.
“I think ChatGPT is better now,” Masad said, adding that the company uses an internal benchmark to help assess metrics like performance. “We publish a public benchmark as well, and consistently, Sol and Astra perform better on building vibe-coded applications from scratch. It was a decision based on both cost, partnership, and performance. And for all these reasons, we just felt like they were a better partner for now.”
It seems, however, that Anthropic hasn’t been completely cut out of Replit, which sells a cloud-based software development platform where users can develop and deploy apps directly from inside their browser.
After Disrupt, Masad clarified that customers can still access Anthropic models on the platform, but that OpenAI’s ChatGPT is now the default. This shift could significantly reduce Replit’s spend with Anthropic since most customers stick with the default rather than selecting a model themselves.
Prior to striking a deal with OpenAI, Replit relied on Anthropic’s Claude models to run its platform. That partnership was said to have
helped
the company grow its revenue significantly, and Masad noted on stage that Replit had been, until recently, a top-five customer of Anthropic’s.
